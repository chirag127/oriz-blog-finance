Margin Trading Facility (MTF) lets you buy more stock than your cash allows: you
put up part of the purchase (your margin), the broker funds the rest, you take
actual delivery of the shares, and those shares sit pledged with the broker as
collateral. You pay **daily interest on the borrowed portion** until you sell or
repay. Unlike intraday, you can hold for weeks or months.

The interest rate is the number that decides whether MTF is a tool or a slow
leak. And it is the number brokers work hardest to blur. Almost every "MTF from
5.99%" or "from 7.99%" banner is a **teaser that applies only to the smallest
slab — usually the first Rs 1 lakh you borrow**. Cross that line and the rate on
the rest jumps to 12–16% a year. This post lays out the actual per-broker slabs,
so you can see exactly what you would pay.

## How MTF interest actually works

The mechanics are consistent across brokers, and understanding them is half the
battle.

- **Charged daily, on the borrowed amount only.** Interest is calculated on the
  funded (broker-lent) portion — not on your own margin, and not on the full
  trade value.
- **Simple interest, debited monthly.** The standard is simple interest:
  `Daily interest = Funded Amount × Annual Rate ÷ 365`. It accrues every calendar
  day (weekends and holidays included) while the position is open, and is
  typically **posted to your ledger monthly**. "Monthly debit" does **not** mean
  monthly compounding — it just means the month's accumulated simple interest is
  booked together.
- **Accrues from T+1.** Interest starts the day after the exchange pay-in (when
  delivery settles) and runs until the day before your sale settles.

**Worked example.** Borrow Rs 1,00,000 at 12% p.a. and hold for 90 days:

- Daily interest = 1,00,000 × 12% ÷ 365 = **Rs 32.88/day**
- 90 days = 32.88 × 90 = **Rs 2,958.90** total
- Debited monthly, that is roughly Rs 986/month on the same principal — no
  compounding.

### The regulatory guardrails (SEBI)

- **Only Group I securities** are MTF-eligible — equity shares and equity ETFs
  classified as Group I. The exact list is published and updated periodically by
  the exchanges (NSE/BSE). If a stock drops out of Group I, its MTF eligibility
  is affected.
- **Margin is exchange-set, not a flat 50%.** The client must fund at least
  **50% initial margin** under the original framework, with **40% maintenance
  margin**. But the true requirement is scrip-specific: Group I F&O stocks need
  VaR + 3×ELM; Group I non-F&O stocks and ETFs need VaR + 5×ELM — so effective
  margin varies by stock and can exceed 50%.
- **Pledge in the depository (T+1).** Funded shares must be pledged in the
  broker's favour via CDSL/NSDL to count as maintenance margin. Members report
  MTF exposure by 6:00 PM on T+1.
- **Mark-to-market, margin call, square-off.** Positions are marked-to-market
  daily. If your margin falls below the maintenance level, the broker must issue
  a **margin call**; if you do not top up, it can **forced-sell (square off)**
  the pledged shares — often at the worst possible moment.
- SEBI floated a **2026 consultation paper reviewing the MTF framework**
  (proposals to rationalise margin requirements), so some of this is under
  review.

## The main event: per-broker slab rates

Rates below are annual (p.a.), charged daily on the borrowed amount. Read the
**band** carefully — the cheap rate almost always applies only to a small first
slab.

### Slab / low-teaser brokers

These lead with a very low headline rate that applies only to the first slice of
borrowing. The real rate for a typical retail position sits far higher.

**Pocketful** — headline "5.99%"

| Borrowing band | Annual rate | Daily rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to Rs 1,00,000 | **5.99%** | 0.0164% |
| Rs 1,00,001 – Rs 25,00,000 | **14.60%** | 0.0400% |
| Above Rs 25,00,000 | **16.00%** | 0.0438% |

- **Max leverage:** up to 5x on 1,200+ stocks.
- **Holding limit:** no fixed limit stated.
- **The trap, spelled out:** 5.99% applies **only to the first Rs 1 lakh
  borrowed**. The band most retail borrowers actually land in (Rs 1L–25L) is
  **14.60% — about 2.4x the headline**, and above Rs 25L it is 16.00%. (An older
  tariff PDF listed the lowest band as "up to Rs 50,000"; the live /mtf page
  supersedes it with "up to Rs 1,00,000".)

**Paytm Money** — headline "7.99%"

| Borrowing band | Annual rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to Rs 1 lakh | **7.99%** |
| Rs 1 lakh – Rs 1 crore | **9.99%** |
| Above Rs 1 crore | **8.99%** |

- **Max leverage:** roughly 4x / ~75–80% funded on approved MTF stocks (Paytm
  does not headline a fixed multiple).
- **Holding limit:** none stated — hold as long as margin is maintained.
- **Other charges:** brokerage on MTF trades is **0.1% of trade value** (capped
  at Rs 20 for trades below Rs 20,000). This replaced a prior flat 11.99% p.a.
  structure. Slab-based pricing has been live since at least September 2025,
  effective as published August 3, 2026.
- **The trap, spelled out:** 7.99% applies **only to the up-to-Rs-1-lakh slab**.
  The practical retail band (Rs 1L–1Cr) is **9.99%** — note this is actually
  *higher* than the above-1-crore slab (8.99%). Paytm deliberately rewards very
  large books; the mid-band is the expensive one. Even so, 9.99% is competitive
  versus most peers, and Paytm's low MTF brokerage helps.

### Discount brokers

**Dhan** — headline "12.49%"

Dhan's ladder is the honest shape: the rate **rises** with the amount borrowed,
so the lowest rate is the best case for the first Rs 5 lakh (not a Rs-1-lakh
teaser).

| Borrowing band | Annual rate | Daily rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to Rs 500 | **Nil** | 0% |
| Rs 500 – Rs 5,00,000 | **12.49%** | 0.0342% |
| Rs 5,00,000 – Rs 10,00,000 | **13.49%** | 0.0369% |
| Rs 10,00,000 – Rs 25,00,000 | **14.49%** | 0.0397% |
| Rs 25,00,000 – Rs 50,00,000 | **15.49%** | 0.0425% |

- **Max leverage:** up to 4x on the official page (some 2026 coverage cites up to
  5x). Total MTF limit up to Rs 5 crore; up to Rs 1 crore per individual stock.
- **Holding limit:** unlimited. Interest runs from T+1 through the day before
  sale settlement. Position is liquidated if holding coverage falls below 20%.
- **Other charges:** interest calculated daily, posted weekly to the ledger.
  1,700+ eligible stocks. You can convert an MTF position to plain delivery.
- **Note:** Dhan's own published table stops at Rs 50 lakh. Third-party sources
  report an "above Rs 50 lakh = 16.49%" tier, but since it is not in Dhan's own
  table, treat it as **unconfirmed — confirm on Dhan's site** if you borrow that
  much.

**m.Stock (Mirae Asset)** — headline "from 8.99%"

m.Stock's cheapest rate sits on the *largest* band, and its most expensive rate
covers the entire retail range — so the effective rate for a normal user is one
of the highest here.

| Borrowing band | Annual rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to Rs 25 lakh | **14.99%** |
| Rs 25 lakh – Rs 5 crore | **9.99%** |
| Above Rs 5 crore | **8.99%** |

- **Max leverage:** up to 80% funding on total trade value (you post 20% margin).
- **Holding limit:** none specified — hold indefinitely while margin is
  maintained. Delivery only (no intraday MTF). ~1,100 eligible stocks.
- **Other charges:** pledge/unpledge Rs 32 per PSN (Pledge Sequence Number) per
  day + GST for Pay Later orders. No MTF subscription fee (bundled with demat).
- **The trap, spelled out:** "from 8.99%" applies **only above Rs 5 crore of
  funding**. A normal retail user (borrowing under Rs 25 lakh) pays **14.99%
  p.a.** — among the highest retail rates in this comparison. m.Stock's own note
  says rates can vary by funding value and holding, so the slab is the governing
  structure. (Article dated May 22, 2026.)

### Full-service & bank brokers

Full-service and bank-backed brokers typically charge more but bundle research,
relationship support, and integrated banking. Rates commonly run **default
around 17.99% p.a., dropping toward ~9.85% only on top-tier paid plans**
(ICICI Direct is the widely cited example: ~9.85% on its highest plan, 17.99% on
the default). Because these plans and rates are plan-specific and change often,
**confirm the exact slab on the broker's official page before relying on any
number** — the pattern to expect is "high default, low only if you pay for a
premium plan or carry a large book".

### Others

Across the wider market in 2026, published MTF rates span roughly **9.85%–18%
p.a.** depending on broker and plan. The shape of the slab matters as much as the
headline: some brokers (Pocketful, Paytm Money) put the cheap rate on a tiny
first slab; others (Dhan) raise the rate as you borrow more; and m.Stock inverts
it, reserving the cheap rate for very large books.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pocketful** | 5.99% (up to Rs 1L) | **14.60%** (Rs 1L–25L) | Steepest teaser; real rate ~2.4x headline. Up to 5x. |

The pattern is unmistakable: **for a normal retail position, Paytm Money's

trader will use.

## The full cost stack beyond interest

Interest is the biggest cost, but not the only one. The full stack:

**Broker-set (vary by broker/plan):**

- **MTF interest** — the 9.85%–~18% p.a. above. The dominant cost on any
  multi-week hold.
- **Brokerage on MTF trades** — Paytm Money charges 0.1% (max Rs 20 on small
  trades); some discount brokers charge a flat per-order fee; free-delivery
  brokers may charge little or nothing. This alone can tilt the "cheapest"
  answer.
- **Pledge / unpledge charges** — typically **~Rs 15–20 + GST per scrip** per
  pledge event at many brokers (m.Stock charges Rs 32 per PSN per day + GST for
  Pay Later, which is on the higher side). Small in absolute terms, but it adds
  up across many positions.
- **DP (depository) charges on sell** — a per-scrip fee when you sell delivery
  shares.

**Statutory (same everywhere — set by government/exchange):**

- **STT** (securities transaction tax) on delivery trades.
- **Stamp duty** on purchase.
- **Exchange transaction charges** (NSE/BSE).
- **SEBI turnover fee.**
- **GST** at 18% on brokerage + exchange charges (not on STT/stamp).

### Worked total-cost example: Rs 2 lakh MTF hold for 1 year

Say you borrow Rs 2,00,000 via MTF and hold for a full year at a 12% effective
rate (a realistic mid-band figure once you cross the teaser slab):

- **Interest:** 2,00,000 × 12% = **Rs 24,000** for the year (Rs ~65.75/day).
- **Brokerage:** at 0.1% on entry and exit of, say, a ~Rs 4 lakh total trade
  value (buy + sell), roughly **Rs 400** + GST — or near zero on a free-delivery
  broker.
- **Pledge/unpledge + DP + statutory charges:** a few hundred rupees, depending
  on scrips and broker.

The headline: **your stock has to rise more than ~12% in a year just to break
even on the interest**, before you count everything else. On a Rs 2 lakh
borrowing, you are handing the broker Rs 24,000 for the year — a real hurdle the
position must clear before you make a paisa.

## Which is genuinely cheapest?

Honest answer: it depends on how much you borrow and for how long.

- **The sub-Rs-1-lakh teaser trick is real but narrow.** With Pocketful (5.99%)
  or Paytm Money (7.99%), you genuinely keep the low rate only if each borrowing
  stays under the lowest slab (Rs 1 lakh). Some traders split across brokers to
  keep each book under its cheap band — but that means multiple accounts,
  multiple pledge fees, and real hassle for a small position. Rarely worth it.
- **For a normal retail position (Rs 1–5 lakh borrowed), Paytm Money's 9.99%
  mid-band is the cheapest confirmed rate here**, and its low 0.1% MTF brokerage
  (free on nothing, but cheap) helps. Dhan's 12.49% rising ladder is next, and
  it is honest about the shape.
- **A flat-rate or bundled-plan broker can win if you borrow large amounts.**
  m.Stock's 9.99% kicks in above Rs 25 lakh and 8.99% above Rs 5 crore; a
  full-service broker's ~9.85% top plan can beat a slab broker's mid-band — but
  only if you actually clear the threshold or pay for the plan.
- **Free / low delivery brokerage matters.** On a big trade value, 0.1%
  brokerage on entry and exit is not trivial. A broker that is slightly costlier
  on interest but free on brokerage can end up cheaper on a short hold.

There is no single "cheapest MTF broker" — there is only the cheapest broker
*for your borrowing size and holding period*. Match the slab to your actual
position.

## Risks and a debt-first reality check

MTF is leverage, and leverage cuts both ways.

- **MTF interest never beats clearing a high-cost debt.** If you are carrying a
  credit-card balance at ~40% a year, no MTF play — even at 8% — is worth it.
  Pay off the card first. Borrowing at 12% to invest while paying 40% on a card
  is setting money on fire.
- **Interest keeps accruing while the stock falls.** You pay every calendar day
  regardless of which way the price moves. A flat or falling stock plus interest
  is a guaranteed loss.
- **Margin calls are brutal.** If the stock drops and your margin falls below the
  maintenance level, the broker issues a call; if you cannot top up, it forced-
  sells your pledged shares — usually near the bottom, locking in the loss.
- **Leverage magnifies both directions.** A 4x position turns a 10% stock move
  into a ~40% swing on your capital. Great on the way up, ruinous on the way
  down.

MTF is for experienced investors with a clear thesis, spare margin capacity, and
the discipline to exit. It is not an emergency fund, not a substitute for cash,
and not a way to "afford" a stock you otherwise cannot.

## Tax note for buy-and-hold (informational)

If you hold the MTF shares and eventually sell:

- **Long-term capital gains** (held > 12 months): taxed at **12.5%** (above the
  annual exemption).
- **Short-term capital gains** (held ≤ 12 months): taxed at **20%**.
- **Interest deductibility** is nuanced. MTF interest is a cost of acquiring/
  holding an investment; whether and how it can be set off depends on your tax
  situation and how you report the activity. This is informational, not tax
  advice — check with a qualified tax professional for your case.

## FAQ

**Is MTF interest charged daily or monthly?**
It **accrues daily** on the borrowed amount (every calendar day, weekends
included) and is typically **debited to your ledger monthly**. Monthly debit is
not monthly compounding — it is just when the accumulated simple interest is
booked.

**Do teaser rates apply to my whole position?**
No — and this is the single biggest trap. Rates like 5.99% or 7.99% apply
**only to the lowest slab** (usually the first Rs 1 lakh borrowed). Anything
above that is charged at the higher band rate (12–16% at most brokers). Your
effective rate is a blend, dominated by the higher slab.

**Can I hold an MTF position forever?**
Most brokers state no fixed holding limit — you can hold as long as you maintain
margin. But interest runs every day, and if your margin coverage drops below the
maintenance threshold the broker can square you off. "Forever" is possible in
theory; expensive in practice.

**Is MTF interest tax-deductible?**
It is nuanced and depends on how you report the investment activity. It is a cost
of holding, but deductibility is not automatic — consult a tax professional
rather than assuming.

**What happens on a margin call?**
If your stock falls and your margin drops below the maintenance level (40% under
the standard framework), the broker issues a margin call asking you to add funds
or securities. If you do not, it can **forced-sell (square off)** your pledged
shares to cover the shortfall — often at a loss.

**Which is the cheapest MTF broker in India?**
For a normal retail position (Rs 1–5 lakh borrowed), **Paytm Money's 9.99%
mid-band is the cheapest confirmed rate** in this comparison, with Dhan's 12.49%
rising ladder next. The "5.99%" and "from 8.99%" headlines apply only to bands
almost no retail trader uses. Confirm current rates on each broker's page — they
change frequently.
