If someone told you the SBI Unnati debit card is "free of cost," they are
**half right** — and the missing half is the part that costs you money. "Free"
usually gets used to mean the card was issued without a fee. Whether it stays
free every year afterwards is a completely separate question, and the answer
depends entirely on **which variant of card is physically sitting in your
wallet**.

There is also a wrinkle most explainers skip: on **SBI's current official
savings-account listing (last updated 13 March 2026), there is no account
called "Unnati" anymore**. It was a real product — a zero/low-balance
financial-inclusion account — but it is absent from SBI's live catalogue today,
having apparently been discontinued or folded into the bank's basic savings
tier. That single fact reshapes the whole question: you cannot pull "Unnati
card" terms off SBI's site in 2026, because SBI no longer publishes them under
that name.

So this post does two honest things. First, it separates the two fees people
constantly conflate — **issuance** (one-time, when the card is made) versus
**annual maintenance charge (AMC)** (recurring, every year). Second, it tells
you **what to actually check on your own card** to know which fee schedule
applies to you.

**Everything here is time-sensitive.** SBI is mid-migration from `sbi.co.in`
to the RBI-mandated `sbi.bank.in` domain, which broke several of its own
service-charge document links during this research. Figures below are
attributed to their source and effective date where known; **confirm the live
number on SBI's own page (now `sbi.bank.in`) before you rely on it.** Where a
figure could not be re-confirmed against a live primary source, it is flagged
as such — treat it as a lead to check, not a fact to bank on.

## The one distinction that answers the whole question

Two words get blurred together constantly. Keep them apart and everything else
falls into place:

| Term | What it means | When you pay it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Issuance fee** | A one-time charge to *make and give you* the card | Once, at the start (many SBI cards: NIL) |
| **Annual maintenance charge (AMC)** | A recurring yearly fee to *keep the card active* | Every year, debited automatically |

"Free of cost" almost always refers to **issuance**. A card can be free to
issue and still carry a recurring AMC. That is precisely the trap with SBI's
standard debit cards — and precisely why the answer for the basic RuPay card is
different from the answer for a Classic-tier card.

## Where "Unnati" actually went

Here is what SBI's own site shows in 2026, and it is the most important context
for anyone holding an Unnati-branded card.

SBI's official savings-account product page (last updated **13 March 2026**)
lists nine savings products: Savings via Video KYC, BSBD (Branch), BSBD, BSBD
Small, the regular Savings Bank Account, Savings for Minors, Savings Plus,
MACT, and RFC Domestic. **None of them is named "Unnati."** The regular Savings
Bank Account, notably, now shows a **Monthly Average Balance requirement of
NIL**.

That page also **does not publish any debit-card issuance fee or annual
maintenance charge at all** — it details only cheque-book charges (first 10
leaves free per financial year; a 10-leaf book at Rs 40 + GST, a 25-leaf book
at Rs 75 + GST) and duplicate-passbook issuance. In other words: **the account
overview page is the wrong place to look for card fees.** Card fees live only
in SBI's separate debit-card service-charges schedule.

The takeaway: **"Unnati" was a genuine historical SBI product, but its live,
name-specific card terms cannot be sourced from SBI today.** If you hold an
existing Unnati card, its treatment now maps to one of SBI's current card
schedules — and the deciding factor is the *card variant*, not the old account
name.

## The two possible answers, side by side

Because "Unnati" no longer appears in SBI's catalogue, your real question is:
*which of SBI's current cards is mine?* There are two materially different
outcomes.

| Your card is… | Issuance | Annual maintenance | Is "free of cost" true? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Basic RuPay ATM-cum-Debit card** (issued with a BSBD / basic zero-balance account) | **Free** | **NIL — none applied** | **Yes, genuinely — both issuance and every year** |
| A **standard variant** (Classic / Silver / Global / Contactless) | Issuance-free | **Rs 200 + GST recurring** (per SBI's service-charges schedule) | **Only for issuance — not in recurring years** |

Everything below unpacks these two rows and how to tell which is yours.

### If your card is the Basic RuPay card: genuinely free

For SBI's basic / zero-balance tier — the **Basic Savings Bank Deposit (BSBD)
account**, which is the closest live equivalent to the old Unnati product —
SBI's official product page states verbatim:

> "Basic RuPay ATM-cum-Debit card will be issued free of cost and no annual
> maintenance charge will be applied."

That is about as unambiguous as it gets. If your card is this **Basic RuPay
ATM-cum-Debit card**, then **"free of cost" is fully accurate — free to issue,
and free to keep, with no AMC in any year.** This finding rests on SBI's own
primary page and was confirmed unanimously in the research.

The BSBD account also comes with **a maximum of 4 free cash withdrawals per
month** (including ATM withdrawals at SBI and other banks' ATMs, branch-channel
withdrawals, and AEPS transactions). That is a real usage constraint on the
basic tier — worth knowing even though it is separate from the card-fee
question.

### If your card is a Classic-tier variant: free to issue, not free to keep

SBI's **standard debit cards — Classic, Silver, Global, and Contactless — carry
a recurring Annual Maintenance Charge of Rs 200 + GST**, per SBI's official
service-charges schedule. Higher variants cost more (broadly:
Yuva/Gold/Combo around Rs 250 + GST, Platinum around Rs 325 + GST, up to
Mastercard World-tier around Rs 800 + GST).

So if your account was issued a standard Classic-tier card rather than the Basic
RuPay card, **"free of cost" holds only for the initial issuance — not for the
years that follow**, where the Rs 200 + GST AMC applies.

**Two honesty flags on this row, stated plainly:**

- **The exact primary PDF could not be opened live this session.** SBI's
  service-charges document (`sbi.co.in/documents/17826/35696/Service+Charges.pdf`)
  now 301-redirects to `sbi.bank.in` and returns a 404 mid-migration, and it
  was never archived by the Wayback Machine. The Rs 200 + GST figure is
  corroborated by a third-party rendering of SBI's schedule (ClearTax) plus
  prior knowledge — this is **medium-confidence**, not a live-primary
  confirmation. Re-check it on the current `sbi.bank.in` service-charges
  document.
- **Do not assume a "first year free" on the AMC.** A widely repeated claim
  says the Rs 200 + GST AMC only kicks in "from the second year onwards" (first
  year free). **That specific mechanism did not survive verification against a
  primary source** and is not asserted here. Treat the AMC as a recurring
  annual charge on standard variants unless SBI's live schedule tells you
  otherwise for your specific card.

## The verdict

**Is the SBI Unnati debit card free of cost? "Partly" — and only you can finish
the sentence, by checking your card variant.**

- **If it is the Basic RuPay ATM-cum-Debit card** (the BSBD basic-tier card),
  then **yes, it is genuinely free** — no issuance fee and no annual
  maintenance charge, per SBI's own page.
- **If it is a standard Classic / Silver / Global / Contactless card**, then it
  is **free to issue but not free to keep** — a recurring **Rs 200 + GST AMC**
  applies (medium-confidence; re-confirm on the live schedule).
- **"Unnati" as a live product name is gone** from SBI's 2026 catalogue, so its
  terms now flow through whichever of the above two schedules matches your
  actual card.

The honest bottom line: **do not accept "it's free" at face value.** Whether
your SBI debit card costs you Rs 0 or Rs 200 + GST every year turns on a single
detail — the card variant — that a blanket "free of cost" claim conveniently
hides.

## What to actually check on your card

You do not need to trust anyone's summary — including this one. Here is the
practical checklist to settle it for your specific card:

1. **Read the variant printed on the card.** Look for the words "Basic RuPay,"
   "Classic," "Silver," "Global," or "Contactless." This one word decides which
   fee schedule applies. "Basic RuPay" = the genuinely-free path; anything
   Classic-tier = the AMC path.
2. **Check the account type it is linked to.** A **BSBD / basic (zero-balance)
   savings account** points to the Basic RuPay, no-AMC outcome. A regular
   savings account is more likely on a standard variant.
3. **Scan your account statement for an AMC debit.** Search your passbook /
   statement history for a **~Rs 200-plus-GST debit** with a card-maintenance
   narration around your card's anniversary month. If you see it, you are on an
   AMC-bearing variant. If a full year has passed with no such debit, you are
   likely on the no-AMC Basic RuPay card.
4. **Pull the current figures from SBI's live schedule.** Because of the
   `sbi.co.in` → `sbi.bank.in` migration, go to SBI's **current** debit-card
   service-charges document on `sbi.bank.in` for the up-to-date AMC,
   replacement, and any PIN/usage charges — and their effective dates.
5. **When in doubt, ask your branch or SBI care** which exact variant your
   account carries and what its recurring AMC is. It is the fastest way to a
   definitive answer for *your* card.

## FAQ

**Is the SBI Unnati account still available in 2026?**
Not under that name on SBI's live catalogue. SBI's official savings-account
listing (last updated 13 March 2026) shows nine savings products, **none named
"Unnati."** It was a real financial-inclusion product that has apparently been
discontinued or merged into the basic savings tier. If you hold an existing
Unnati card, its fees now map to whichever current SBI card variant it is.

**Is the SBI debit card free to get?**
For the **Basic RuPay ATM-cum-Debit card** issued with a BSBD account, yes —
free to issue *and* no annual maintenance charge, per SBI's own page. Standard
variants (Classic/Silver/Global/Contactless) are also **issuance-free**, but
they carry a recurring AMC (Rs 200 + GST on the Classic tier, per SBI's
service-charges schedule).

**How much is the annual maintenance charge?**
On SBI's standard Classic-tier cards, **Rs 200 + GST** per year, per SBI's
service-charges schedule (medium-confidence — the live primary PDF could not be
re-opened during this research due to SBI's domain migration; re-confirm on
`sbi.bank.in`). The **Basic RuPay card has no AMC.** Higher variants cost more
(Gold/Combo ~Rs 250 + GST, Platinum ~Rs 325 + GST, and up).

**Does the AMC skip the first year?**
Unverified — and deliberately not claimed here. A common "first year free, AMC
from year two" statement **did not survive verification** against a primary
source. Treat the AMC on standard variants as a recurring annual charge unless
SBI's current schedule says otherwise for your card.

**What does a replacement card cost?**
SBI's schedule lists **Rs 300 + GST for "All Debit Cards"** (medium-confidence;
re-confirm on the live `sbi.bank.in` schedule).

**Are there PIN-regeneration or per-transaction charges?**
Not established by verified sources in this research, so no figure is given
here. The only confirmed usage limit is the BSBD account's **4 free cash
withdrawals per month** — an account-level rule, not a card fee. Check SBI's
current schedule for anything more.

**Why can't I find SBI's charges PDF?**
SBI is migrating from `sbi.co.in` to the RBI-mandated `sbi.bank.in` domain, and
the old service-charges PDF link 301-redirects and then 404s. Use SBI's current
`sbi.bank.in` service-charges page for the live document.
